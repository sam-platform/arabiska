الجمعة عند تيتا

Friday at Grandma's
اللهجة الفلسطينية  —  القدس
Spoken Palestinian (Jerusalem). No fus7a, no tashkeel.

Before you start

This one is about family, food, and an old man remembering. It is where you meet the past tense properly, plus the trick for saying "we used to".

The lovely bit. A grandparent calls the grandchild by their own title: سيدو says تعال يا جدي ta3aal ya jiddi — literally "come, my grandfather" — meaning "come here, my boy". Same with يا ماما from a mother to her child. It sounds backwards and it is completely normal.
1

كل جمعة، عيلة سامي بتروح عند تيتا في بيت حنينا.

تيتا بتفتح الباب وبتقول:

«يا هلا! يا هلا! تفضلوا!»

kill jum3a, 3eelit Saami btrou7 3ind Teta fee Beet 7aneena. Teta btifta7 il-baab w-bit2oul: “ya hala! ya hala! tfaddalu!”

Every Friday, Sami's family goes to Grandma's in Beit Hanina. Grandma opens the door and says: “Welcome! Welcome! Come in!”

2

ريحة المقلوبة بتملا البيت كله.

تيتا بتقول:

«اليوم عملتلكم مقلوبة، وسلطة، وشوية لبن.»

ree7it il-ma2loube btimla il-beet killo. Teta bit2oul: “il-yom 3amaltilkom ma2loube, w-salata, w-shwayyit laban.”

The smell of maqluba fills the whole house. Grandma says: “Today I've made you maqluba, salad, and a bit of yoghurt.”

3

«تيتا، ليش اسمها مقلوبة؟»

تيتا بتضحك وبتقول:

«لأنه منقلبها عالطبق يا سيدي! شوف!»

وبتقلب الطنجرة... طاخ!

“Teta, leesh ismha ma2loube?” Teta btid7ak w-bit2oul: “la2inno mnu2libha 3at-taba2 ya seedi! shouf!” w-bti2leb it-tanjara... taa5!

“Grandma, why is it called maqluba?” Grandma laughs and says: “Because we flip it over onto the plate, my dear! Watch!” And she flips the pot... thud!

4

بعد الأكل، سيدو بيقعد عالبلكونة وبيشرب قهوة.

بيقول لسامي:

«تعال يا جدي، خليني احكيلك حكاية من زمان.»

ba3d il-akel, Seedo byi23od 3al-balkone w-byishrab 2ahwe. bi2oul la-Saami: “ta3aal ya jiddi, 5alleeni a7keelak 7ikaaye min zamaan.”

After the meal, Grandpa sits out on the balcony and drinks coffee. He says to Sami: “Come here, my boy, let me tell you a story from the old days.”

5

«لما كنت زغير، ما كان في تلفزيون ولا موبايل.

كنا نلعب بالحارة لحد ما تصير الدنيا عتمة.»

سامي بيسأل:

«وشو كنتوا تلعبوا؟»

“lamma kunt zgheer, maa kaan fee television wala mobile. kunna nil3ab bil-7aara la7ad ma tseer id-dinya 3atme.” Saami byis2al: “w-shou kuntu til3abu?”

“When I was little, there was no TV and no mobile. We used to play out in the neighbourhood until it went dark.” Sami asks: “And what did you use to play?”

6

«كنا نلعب غميضة، وسبع حجار، وكرة.

وإمي كانت تنادي من الشباك:

يلا عالبيت! تأخرتوا!»

“kunna nil3ab ghmeeda, w-sab3 7jaar, w-koora. w-immi kaanat tnaadi min ish-shibbaak: yalla 3al-beet! t2a55artu!”

“We used to play hide-and-seek, seven stones, and football. And my mother would call from the window: Come on, get home! You're late!”

7

عالليل، وهم راجعين عالبيت، سامي قال لأبوه:

«بابا، بدي أروح عند تيتا كل يوم، مش بس يوم الجمعة.»

أبوه ضحك وقال:

«وبعدين مين رح يوكل المقلوبة كلها؟ إنت؟»

3al-leel, w-humme raj3een 3al-beet, Saami 2aal la-abou: “baaba, biddi arou7 3ind Teta kill yom, mish bass yom ij-jum3a.” abou di7ik w-2aal: “w-ba3deen meen ra7 yokel il-ma2loube killha? inte?”

At night, on their way home, Sami said to his dad: “Dad, I want to go to Grandma's every day, not just on Friday.” His dad laughed and said: “And then who's going to eat that whole maqluba? You?”

Dialect notes

The past tense, and the one construction that lets you talk about your childhood.

1 · Past tense endings

Take the bare past stem — لعب li3ib, he played — and add:

لعبتli3ibtI played / you (m.) played
لعبتيli3ibtiyou (f.) played
لعبli3ibhe played
لعبتli3batshe played
لعبناli3ibnawe played
لعبتواli3ibtuyou (pl.) played
لعبواli3buthey played

No بـ in the past — that prefix belongs to the present only.

2 · كان + present = "used to"

Put كان in front of a present-tense verb (drop the بـ) and you get the habitual past — the tense of every childhood story:
كنت ألعب kunt al3ab I used to play · كنا نلعب kunna nil3ab we used to play · كانت تنادي kaanat tnaadi she would call.
Keep the بـ and it means something else: كنت بلعب kunt bal3ab — I was playing.

Dialect notes · continued

 

3 · كان makes any sentence past

Palestinian has no present-tense "to be": أنا تعبان ana ta3baan — I'm tired. To put it in the past, just add كان:
كنت تعبان kunt ta3baan I was tired · كان في kaan fee there was · ما كان في maa kaan fee there wasn't · كان عندي kaan 3indi I had.
