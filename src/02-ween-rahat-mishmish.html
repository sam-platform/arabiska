وين راحت مشمش؟
ween raa7at Mishmish?
Where Did Mishmish Go?
اللهجة الفلسطينية  —  القدس
Spoken Palestinian (Jerusalem). No fus7a, no tashkeel.

Before you start

Same system as book 1: Arabic first, then the Jerusalem pronunciation, then English. This story is built around asking questions — the words on this page are the ones you will use every single day.

Word order. Unlike fus7a, the question word usually goes exactly where the answer would go, and you do not restructure the sentence: رحت وين؟ ru7t ween? "you went where?" is just as normal as وين رحت؟ ween ru7t?
1

عند سامي قطة اسمها مشمش.

لونها أبيض وبرتقالي.

مشمش بتحب تنام عالشباك وتتفرج عالناس.

3ind Saami 2atta ismha Mishmish. lonha abyad w-burtu2aani. Mishmish bit7ibb tnaam 3ash-shibbaak w-titfarraj 3an-naas.

Sami has a cat called Mishmish (“Apricot”). She's white and orange. Mishmish likes sleeping on the window and watching the people go by.

2

يوم من الأيام، الشباك كان مفتوح.

مشمش قامت، مطت حالها، ونطت برا!

yom min il-ayyaam, ish-shibbaak kaan maftou7. Mishmish 2aamat, mattat 7aalha, w-nattat barra!

One day, the window was open. Mishmish got up, had a good stretch, and jumped outside!

3

سامي رجع من المدرسة ونادى:

«مشمش! مشمش! وينك؟»

دور تحت الطاولة... ما في.

دور جوا الخزانة... ولا إشي.

Saami rija3 min il-madrase w-naada: “Mishmish! Mishmish! weenek?” dawwar ta7t it-taawle... maa fee. dawwar juwwa il-5zaane... wala ishi.

Sami came home from school and called out: “Mishmish! Mishmish! Where are you?” He searched under the table... nothing. He searched inside the cupboard... nothing at all.

4

نزل عالشارع وسأل الجيران:

«لو سمحتي يا خالة، شفتي قطة بيضا وبرتقالي؟»

«لأ يا حبيبي، ما شفتها. بس جرب عند الدكانة.»

nizil 3ash-shaari3 w-sa2al ij-jeeraan: “law sama7ti ya 5aale, shufti 2atta beeda w-burtu2aani?” “la2 ya 7abeebi, maa shuftha. bass jarrib 3ind id-dukkaane.”

He went down to the street and asked the neighbours: “Excuse me, auntie, have you seen a white and orange cat?” “No, love, I haven't seen her. But try at the shop.”

5

راح عند الدكانة. صاحب الدكانة قله:

«في قطة صغيرة قاعدة فوق عالسطح من الصبح.

وعم بتموء كتير.»

raa7 3ind id-dukkaane. saa7ib id-dukkaane 2allo: “fee 2atta zgheere 2aa3de fo2 3as-sat7 min is-sob7. w-3am btmoo2 kteer.”

He went to the shop. The shopkeeper said to him: “There's a little cat sitting up on the roof since this morning. And she's meowing a lot.”

6

سامي طلع عالسطح، وشو شاف؟

مشمش قاعدة عالحيط، وحدها، وخايفة تنزل.

Saami tili3 3as-sat7, w-shou shaaf? Mishmish 2aa3de 3al-7eet, wa7adha, w-5aayfe tinzal.

Sami went up to the roof, and what did he see? Mishmish sitting on the wall, all by herself, too scared to come down.

7

«تعالي يا مشمش، ما تخافي، أنا هون.»

حملها ونزل، وهي عم تخرخر عالطريق.

ومن هديك اليوم، سامي بيسكر الشباك قبل ما يروح عالمدرسة.

“ta3aali ya Mishmish, maa t5aafi, ana hoon.” 7amalha w-nizil, w-hiyye 3am t5ar5er 3at-taree2. w-min hadeek il-yom, Saami bisakker ish-shibbaak 2abel ma yrou7 3al-madrase.

“Come here, Mishmish, don't be scared, I'm here.” He picked her up and carried her down, and she purred the whole way. And from that day on, Sami shuts the window before he leaves for school.

Dialect notes

Three things this story quietly drilled into you.

1 · There is no verb "to have"

You use عند 3ind plus a pronoun ending:
عندي 3indi I have · عندك 3indak / 3indek you have (m./f.) · عندو 3indo he has · عندها 3indha she has · عنا 3inna we have · عندهم 3indhom they have.
Negative: ما عندي maa 3indi — I don't have.

2 · The endings that do everything

The same little endings mark possession on a noun and the object on a verb. Learn them once and they work everywhere.

ـي-imy / me — اسمي ismi my name
ـك-ak (m.) / -ek (f.)your / you — وينك weenak, weenek
ـه-ohis / him — قله 2allo he told him
ـها-haher — حملها 7amalha he carried her
ـنا-naour / us — جبلنا jiblna bring us
ـكم-komyour / you (pl.) — بيتكم beetkom your house
ـهم-homtheir / them — خدهم 5idhom take them

Dialect notes · continued

 

3 · Telling someone to do something

The imperative is short and blunt, and you soften it with لو سمحت or by adding يا plus a name.
روح / روحي / روحوا rou7 / rou7i / rou7u — go (m. / f. / pl.)
تعال / تعالي / تعالوا ta3aal / ta3aali / ta3aalu — come
شوف / شوفي / شوفوا shouf / shoufi / shoufu — look
Negative commands use ما plus the present, no بـ: ما تخاف maa t5aaf don't be afraid · ما تنسى maa tinsa don't forget.

A word about ـش. In Gaza, Hebron and much of the south you will hear negation wrapped around the verb: ما بعرفش maa ba3rafsh. In Jerusalem, Ramallah, and up through Jordan and Syria, people just say ما بعرف maa ba3ref. Understand both; produce the second.
